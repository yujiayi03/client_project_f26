Ending Your Experience

Close Your Project with Care

How you end an engaged learning project matters. A responsible closeout ensures that your partner can use what you created, your team learns from the experience, and you can articulate the value of the work you completed.

Why Responsible Closeout Matters

The final phase of an engaged learning project is not just about delivering a product—it is about leaving the partnership in a good position for the future. A thoughtful handoff, honest reflection, and clear documentation set the foundation for the partner to continue the work and for future students to build on what you started.

The skills you practice in this phase—synthesizing experience, communicating impact, and managing transitions—are the same skills employers value in new graduates.

Delivering Your Work

The handoff of project deliverables is a critical moment. How you package, document, and transfer your work determines whether the partner can actually use what you created.

Handoff Best Practices

  • Document everything: Provide clear documentation for any code, designs, data, or processes the partner will need to maintain or extend.
  • Organize files: Use a logical folder structure and naming conventions so the partner can find what they need.
  • Provide a walkthrough: Schedule a session to walk the partner through the deliverables, answer questions, and demonstrate how to use them.
  • Identify next steps: Be clear about what is complete, what is still in progress, and what was not addressed in the project.

Questions to Consider

  1. Can the partner use this without us? If your team disappeared tomorrow, could the organization maintain and use what you created?
  2. What documentation is needed? What does the partner need to know to understand, use, and modify the deliverables?
  3. What was not completed? Be transparent about what the project did not address and why.
  4. What would you do differently? Reflect on what the team learned and what advice you would give to future students.

Reflecting on Your Experience

Reflection is a core component of engaged learning. Taking time to think critically about what you experienced, what you learned, and how you grew helps you integrate the experience into your broader education and career development.

Reflection Prompts

  • What did you learn? Consider technical skills, professional competencies, and personal insights you gained from the experience.
  • What challenged you? Identify moments that pushed you outside your comfort zone and how you responded.
  • How did your understanding change? Reflect on how the project affected your understanding of the community, the issue, or the field.
  • What would you do differently? Think about what you would change if you could do the project again.

Connecting to Your Goals

Consider how this experience connects to your academic and professional goals. What skills do you want to continue developing? What areas of interest did the project reveal? How can you build on this experience in future courses, projects, or career opportunities?

Communicating Your Impact

Being able to articulate what you accomplished and what you learned is essential for resumes, portfolios, interviews, and professional networking.

Building Your Narrative

  • Describe the context: What was the project, who was the partner, and what problem were you addressing?
  • Explain your role: What specifically did you contribute? What skills did you use or develop?
  • Share the outcome: What did the project accomplish? What impact did it have on the partner or community?
  • Connect to growth: How did this experience change you as a professional or as a person?

Updating Your Materials

After completing an engaged learning experience, update your resume, portfolio, and LinkedIn profile to reflect what you accomplished. Use specific, concrete language to describe your contributions and their impact.
