Starting Your Experience

Launch Your Project

Once you have prepared yourself and your team, it is time to launch the project. Starting well means establishing clear expectations, building a productive relationship with your partner, and setting up systems that will support your work throughout the experience.

Why a Strong Start Matters

The first interactions with your partner organization set the tone for the entire project. A thoughtful approach to building the partnership helps establish trust, clarify expectations, and create a foundation for productive work.

When you invest time in establishing clear communication norms, defining roles, and aligning on goals at the outset, you prevent misunderstandings and set your team up for success.

Phase 2: Starting the Experience

With preparation complete, it is time to begin the actual work of the project. This phase focuses on building the partnership, establishing systems, and setting the stage for productive collaboration.

Building the Partnership

The first interactions with your partner organization set the tone for the entire project. Approach these early conversations with curiosity, humility, and a genuine desire to understand their needs and constraints.

Your goal is to move from a tentative agreement to a shared understanding of what success looks like for everyone involved.

Starting Goals

  • Establish communication norms: Agree on how the team and partner will communicate, how often, and through which channels.
  • Clarify roles and responsibilities: Confirm who is doing what, both within the team and between the team and the partner organization.
  • Define project scope: Document what the project will and will not include, what deliverables are expected, and what timeline you are working within.
  • Set up project management tools: Choose and configure the tools you will use for task tracking, file sharing, and collaboration.

Questions to Consider

  1. What does the partner need? Listen to their description of the problem, their constraints, and what they hope to achieve.
  2. What does success look like? Ask the partner to describe what a successful project outcome would mean for them and their stakeholders.
  3. What constraints exist? Learn about time, budget, technology, and organizational limitations that will shape the work.
  4. Who are the stakeholders? Identify everyone who will be affected by or have input into the project.
  5. How will you handle changes? Discuss how the team and partner will manage scope changes, unexpected challenges, and shifting priorities.
