During Your Experience

Navigate the Middle of Your Project

The middle of an engaged learning project is often the most complex stage. You are actively working with your partner, managing team dynamics, adapting to new information, and problem-solving in real time. This is where preparation meets practice.

Why the Middle Matters

Engaged learning projects rarely follow a linear path. New constraints emerge, stakeholder needs evolve, and teams must adapt their plans while maintaining momentum. The middle of the project is where you develop the professional skills that will serve you throughout your career: communication, adaptability, ethical reasoning, and collaborative problem-solving.

How you navigate challenges during this phase—how you communicate setbacks, adjust scope, and maintain trust with your partner—often matters more than whether everything went according to plan.

Managing Your Team

Effective team management is essential for project success. As work intensifies, clear communication and defined processes help your team stay aligned and productive.

Team Coordination Goals

  • Maintain regular check-ins: Hold consistent team meetings to review progress, address concerns, and adjust plans as needed.
  • Track progress transparently: Use shared tools to document task status, decisions, and upcoming deadlines.
  • Address conflicts early: When disagreements or tensions arise, address them directly and constructively before they escalate.
  • Support team wellbeing: Recognize when team members are overwhelmed and redistribute work or adjust timelines when possible.

Questions to Consider

  1. Is the team communicating effectively? Are meetings productive? Is information flowing between all members?
  2. Are roles still clear? Has anyone taken on too much or too little? Do responsibilities need to be adjusted?
  3. What obstacles have emerged? Identify blockers and determine what support or resources the team needs.
  4. How is the partnership going? Is the relationship with the partner organization productive and respectful?

Working with Your Partner

Your relationship with the partner organization is central to the project success. Maintaining trust, transparency, and mutual respect throughout the work is essential.

Communication Best Practices

  • Provide regular updates: Keep the partner informed about progress, challenges, and changes even when the news is not all positive.
  • Ask for feedback: Create opportunities for the partner to share concerns, ask questions, and guide the direction of the work.
  • Document agreements: When decisions are made or scope is adjusted, confirm the agreement in writing to prevent misunderstandings.
  • Be responsive: Reply to partner communications promptly and follow through on commitments.

Managing Scope Changes

Scope changes are common in engaged learning projects. New information, shifting priorities, or unexpected constraints may require adjustments to the original plan.

When changes arise, discuss them openly with the partner and your team. Document what is changing, why, and how it affects the timeline, deliverables, and responsibilities.

Ethical Decision-Making

Engaged learning projects often involve ethical considerations: how you collect and use data, who benefits from the work, and how you represent the community or organization you are working with.

Ethical Considerations

  • Informed consent: Ensure that anyone participating in research or providing data understands how it will be used.
  • Data privacy: Protect sensitive information and follow institutional and legal requirements for data handling.
  • Representation: Be thoughtful about how you describe the community, organization, and the people involved in your project.
  • Power dynamics: Consider how your position as a student and outsider may affect the partnership and the work.
